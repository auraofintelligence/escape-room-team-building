Under the assumed six-player limit, four rooms would accommodate twenty-four paying players at once. A fifth room takes game capacity to thirty, but it also changes reflection, supervision and circulation requirements. A class of thirty can instead use two cohorts of fifteen in three teams of five, consuming six room sessions with a supervised parallel activity.

A reusable physical and story system

Room concepts worth comparing include practical coordination, investigation, shared-place decisions and journeys through changing conditions. Parallel tasks followed by a combined decision are one possible design pattern, rather than a settled room catalogue. Different participants can contribute information, observation, making, organising or explanation.

Independent play without hints is a design direction retained from the project brief. A room would need its necessary clues and instructions inside the game. Once play starts there are no hints from staff or AI, including vocabulary or teamwork prompts. Staff handle safety, access and equipment faults directly. The hard stop and reflection remain part of the experience even when the group has unfinished work.

Use replaceable puzzle cassettes, accessible service panels, labelled wiring, robust connectors, reusable furniture and a consistent reset state. Scenic elements can vary by place without changing every technical component. Each scenario has age and access settings, a parts list, solution record, reset checklist, fault response, version and independent-play test record.

The room cycle and people onsite

Worked illustration | 29 October 2026 | Quantities and business inputs are assumptions for comparison.

This worked timetable, dated 29 October 2026, assumes 10 minutes of briefing, 45 minutes of play, 25 minutes of reflection and 10 minutes of transitions: a 90-minute visit. A separate 60-minute room cycle assumes play, 10 minutes of reset and 5 minutes of buffer. None of these durations is selected or validated by operating data.

With four rooms, starts staggered by 15 minutes and eight starts per room, the arithmetic gives 32 offered team sessions per day. At six days a week across 48 weeks, it gives 9,216 nominal sessions before the separately modelled renewal closures. Offered sessions are capacity, not bookings.

That pattern could overlap three games and two reflections. It therefore raises questions about reception, monitoring, reset work, facilitation, breaks and access support. Annual task hours cannot establish whether a workable roster exists. Simultaneous school groups would need a separate timetable and space test.

A second illustration uses a 75-minute room cycle: 45 minutes of play, 15 minutes of reset and 15 minutes of buffer. Seven starts per room and five additional paid reset minutes per team are assumed in its calculation. The 45-minute workshop extension is another input to test. Alternative durations, staffing and unrecorded reflection remain open comparisons.

Audio, controls and the room record

Recording is a technical option to examine for post-play reflection. Research questions include speech capture during movement, overlapping conversation, speaker attribution, useful timestamps, preparation time and whether an unrecorded or simpler recording approach meets the same purpose. Participant choices and control of their records are part of the design.

The priced single-room consumer wireless basket is a technical and cost comparison. It does not establish a multi-room radio design. Acoustic isolation, background audio, microphone placement, cleaning, charging, storage and human review would need testing against the room format. Game controls would need to work independently of any transcription service.

A spatial record, asset register and version history could support installation, resets and maintenance. The business records and a participant's personal Aura have different purposes and access requirements. The architecture and software to connect them remain research questions.

Just-in-time learning for installation and operations

Just-in-time learning is a core aim. Methods to compare include drawings, short demonstrations, 360-degree video and the skilled worker's explanation of a task. Associate each lesson with its component, conditions, tools, steps, checks and result. Useful faults and corrections become part of the learning material.

A labourer can receive the specific lesson for labelling a module, fitting a scenic panel, preparing a reset kit or checking a documented starting state. Licensed and specialist work remains assigned to the people authorised and competent to perform it. A recorded lesson supports the work and its supervision.

A useful learning record would distinguish observation, rehearsal, performance and acceptance of each task. Include paid learning, filming, editing, mentoring and handover in the work package. The next crew receives an improved method and the changes that apply to its actual site.
