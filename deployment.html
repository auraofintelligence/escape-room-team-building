Room design and deployment

Build a complete operating room, then turn each installation into practical knowledge for the next paid crew.

Three deployment formats

FormatPhysical scopeCommercial purpose
Hosted two-room pilotTwo game modules, selected at 40 to 50 m² each, plus measured shared briefing, reflection, storage and access facilitiesEstablish the complete visitor, reset, training and cost record with a host.
Four-room reference venueFour selected 45 m² games with dedicated reflection and support spaces in a 400 m² programmeServe several segments across the week and support a broader repeat catalogue.
Partner expansion or room replacementOne or more measured modules integrated into a working host venueAdd a new experience or rotate content without rebuilding the whole business.

The project register records the actual host, property owner, operator, room owner and contracting seller. Those roles can belong to different entities. Capacity is calculated from games, people, cycle times and staffed hours. The premises is selected against that brief.

The selected game size falls within the supplier examples in the market chapter. The other areas describe the intended operation. A measured layout and building assessment determine the final dimensions, usable capacity and works. The priced equipment basket does not imply that any unselected building already contains these spaces.

Four six-player rooms accommodate twenty-four paying players at once. A fifth room takes game capacity to thirty, but it also changes reflection, supervision and circulation requirements. A class of thirty can instead use two cohorts of fifteen in three teams of five, consuming six room sessions with a supervised parallel activity.

A reusable physical and story system

Four initial room families cover practical coordination, investigation, shared-place decisions and a journey through changing conditions. Each uses three parallel task paths before a combined decision. Different participants can contribute information, observation, making, organising or explanation.

The game contains its necessary clues and instructions. Once play starts there are no hints from staff or AI, including vocabulary or teamwork prompts. Staff handle safety, access and equipment faults directly. The hard stop and reflection remain part of the experience even when the group has unfinished work.

Use replaceable puzzle cassettes, accessible service panels, labelled wiring, robust connectors, reusable furniture and a consistent reset state. Scenic elements can vary by place without changing every technical component. Each scenario has age and access settings, a parts list, solution record, reset checklist, fault response, version and independent-play test record.

The room cycle and people onsite

The reference product uses 10 minutes of briefing, 45 minutes of play, 25 minutes of reflection and 10 minutes of transitions: a 90-minute visit. The game room itself has a 60-minute cycle, with 10 minutes of reset and 5 minutes of buffer after play. This is a selected product format; the market sample also contains longer games.

Stagger four room starts by 15 minutes. Eight starts per room provide 32 offered team sessions per day. Six days across 48 trading weeks provide 9,216 offered sessions a year. These are timetable opportunities. Sold sessions and actual group sizes determine attendance and sales.

The ordinary stagger has up to three games active and two reflections at once. Peak roles are reception and briefing, game monitoring, reset and technical work, and two reflection facilitators. Premium workshops, breaks, younger children and specific support needs require their own staffed coverage. A simultaneous school start requires a different reflection roster.

The workshop room has a separate calendar. A premium workshop adds 45 minutes after the standard visit. School holding activities and premium workshops cannot be promised the same space at the same time. A slower 75-minute alternative uses 45 minutes of play, 15 minutes of reset and 15 minutes of buffer. Its cost model pays for the five additional reset minutes.

Audio, controls and the room record

The prototype tests speech capture during movement and overlapping conversation. Speaker attribution and room-event timestamps support the facilitator's review. Game controls remain independent of the transcription service so a processing interruption does not stop play.

A single-room consumer wireless demonstration does not establish a four-room radio system. The itemised budget separates the one-room capture prototype from the multi-room RF, acoustic and integration work package. Acoustic isolation, sound absorption, background audio, microphone placement, cleaning, charging and spare equipment are included in the technical brief.

Each deployed room has an asset register and spatial record covering its layout, interfaces, operating state, maintenance, warranties and versions. The room record, booking ledger, business finances and participant's private Aura are distinct records with authorised connections.

Just-in-time learning for installation and operations

Capture selected work with 360-degree video, appropriate wearable or handheld views, drawings and the skilled worker's explanation. Associate each lesson with its component, conditions, tools, steps, checks and result. Useful faults and corrections become part of the learning material.

A labourer can receive the specific lesson for labelling a module, fitting a scenic panel, preparing a reset kit or checking a documented starting state. Licensed and specialist work remains assigned to the people authorised and competent to perform it. A recorded lesson supports the work and its supervision.

The deployment record shows who observed, rehearsed, performed and accepted each task. Include paid learning, filming, editing, mentoring and handover in the work package. The next crew receives an improved method and the changes that apply to its actual site.
