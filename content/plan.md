# Escape Room Team Building

An Australian network business plan

Luke Nathan Hayes | 29 October 2026 | Business plan 1.0

## Executive summary

Build a national adventure network that brings visitors to local businesses, turns shared challenges into skills for life, and gives Australians a fair go in the age of superintelligence.

### The business

I am building an Australian escape-room network alongside Aura of Intelligence and p4a-xyz-cinema. The business designs and deploys distinctive rooms, helps local operators run them, connects those experiences into tourism routes, and renews the catalogue so groups have a reason to return. Visitors buy an enjoyable shared adventure. Schools and employers can buy a deeper programme, and participating venues gain a route to repeat bookings, new customers and continuing practical support.

I lead the business with a proposed suite of AI agents. Paid labourers, tradespeople, designers, facilitators and specialist contractors carry out the work that needs their skills. The agents prepare research, specifications, schedules, comparisons, records and decision packs. The operating system described in this plan awaits my approval before any role is activated or given authority to contact people, hire, purchase or operate customer services.

Joyful Responsible Abundance gives the business its direction: experiences worth enjoying, responsibility for our effect on other people, and wider access to useful work, learning, ownership and opportunity.

Sources: P01, P10, P14

### What customers and partners buy

The opening product catalogue has a $39 school and youth visit, a $59 public visit and a $99 facilitated team workshop, all proposed consumer prices including GST. A $169 direct three-room network pass connects different experiences and destinations. The pass is a proposed commercial product, with a specific venue-settlement model later in this plan.

Local businesses can participate through a hosted room, an independently operated venue, a room-deployment contract or a tourism partnership. Co-operatives can own local assets where that suits their members. Accommodation, food, cultural experiences, transport and other activities remain distinct businesses with agreed responsibilities and payments.

The revenue model has three ledgers: local venue admissions and workshops; paid design, installation and renewal contracts; and separately agreed network services and distribution. Each ledger records its own payer, delivery cost and margin. Visitor spending at a partner cafe is valuable local activity and remains that cafe's revenue.

Sources: D07, P03, P05

### Why this is a commercial opportunity

Australia already has an established escape-room market. The operator sample in this plan ranges from three to fourteen rooms at one venue, alongside larger mixed attractions. Existing operators sell corporate events, multiple-game packages and touring or relocated game content. The opportunity is to combine good rooms, useful reflection, repeat progression, local stories and an intentionally connected visitor network.

Tourism, school excursions, public leisure and workplace development serve different parts of the calendar. Each candidate location gets its own demand case, built from reachable customers, competitor offers, partner accommodation, transport, opening hours and actual bookings. National tourism and education statistics supply context. Deployment follows local commercial cases and available delivery capacity.

Sources: M01, M03, M07, M09, M10, M16

## A tourism network that rewards return visits

Give every destination its own character and every returning group a fresh reason to travel, contribute and learn.

### The network product

A visitor can enjoy one room as a complete experience, then choose a linked journey through other participating venues. Each room has a different story, task structure and local connection. The network maintains room and scenario versions so returning groups can find genuinely unfamiliar experiences.

Three product lengths serve different customers: a local day with two experiences and an independent food or cultural stop; a regional weekend with several rooms and accommodation; and a longer passport used across a journey. Employer retreats add a workshop tied to actual work. School series can revisit the same capabilities through different room settings.

Partner businesses publish their actual offer, price, capacity, accessibility and booking arrangements. A package can be sold by a named contracting operator, or customers can buy linked services directly. The selling and settlement model is clear before bookings open.

Sources: D07, M06, M09, M22

### A proposed three-destination journey

| Stage | Experience and local trade | Skill carried into the next setting |
|---|---|---|
| First destination | A practical workshop room, followed by an independently operated lunch or gallery visit | Share information, make a clear request and notice assumptions. |
| Second destination | An investigation room in another town, with accommodation and local experiences available through partners | Check evidence, hear another interpretation and revise a plan. |
| Third destination | A shared-place room and optional final reflection | Negotiate responsibilities, repair an unhelpful exchange and choose a real-life application. |

Brisbane, Redlands and Minjerribah provide one route concept for development. Sunshine Coast and hinterland, Northern Rivers and Coffs Harbour, and Melbourne, Beechworth and Albury provide other possible regional patterns. These are development examples. Actual participating venues, ferry or road timetables, accessible transport, cultural hosts and accommodation must be assembled for each published itinerary.

Travel itself creates useful practice: agreeing a meeting time, accommodating different needs, asking for assistance, changing a plan and sharing responsibility. The next room introduces a fresh task where people can use their skills without an in-game reminder.

Sources: D07, P05, M16

### The direct network pass and settlement

The proposed direct pass is $169 per person for three participating rooms. Its allocation is $53 to each receiving venue and $10 for the network service, all stated as GST-inclusive cash amounts where applicable. Compared with three individual $59 admissions, the pass saves $8 and offers a connected journey.

The venue allocations remain committed to future admissions until the relevant service is delivered or the booking is otherwise resolved under the agreed terms. A venue records its own admission revenue. The network records its service amount under its agreed agency or principal arrangement. Accounting and tax treatment are confirmed in the operating agreements.

For five passes bought in one domestic-card transaction, $845 is collected, $795 is allocated to venue admissions and $50 to the network service. Payment processing and the network's own delivery costs come out of the network amount in this example. The finance chapter shows the calculation and the limits of this tariff.

Wholesale and high-commission channels need a separately priced offer. The direct pass cannot absorb every distribution fee while preserving the same venue payments. Refunds, cancellations, unredeemed admissions, partner departure and failed delivery each have an assigned payer and settlement procedure.

Sources: M05, M16

### A reusable physical and story system

Four initial room families cover practical coordination, investigation, shared-place decisions and a journey through changing conditions. Each uses three parallel task paths before a combined decision. Different participants can contribute information, observation, making, organising or explanation.

The game contains its necessary clues and instructions. Once play starts there are no hints from staff or AI, including vocabulary or teamwork prompts. Staff handle safety, access and equipment faults directly. The hard stop and reflection remain part of the experience even when the group has unfinished work.

Use replaceable puzzle cassettes, accessible service panels, labelled wiring, robust connectors, reusable furniture and a consistent reset state. Scenic elements can vary by place without changing every technical component. Each scenario has age and access settings, a parts list, solution record, reset checklist, fault response, version and independent-play test record.

Sources: D05, D14

### The room cycle and people onsite

The reference product uses 10 minutes of briefing, 45 minutes of play, 25 minutes of reflection and 10 minutes of transitions: a 90-minute visit. The game room itself has a 60-minute cycle, with 10 minutes of reset and 5 minutes of buffer after play. This is a selected product format; the market sample also contains longer games.

Stagger four room starts by 15 minutes. Eight starts per room provide 32 offered team sessions per day. Six days across 48 trading weeks provide 9,216 offered sessions a year. These are timetable opportunities. Sold sessions and actual group sizes determine attendance and sales.

The ordinary stagger has up to three games active and two reflections at once. Peak roles are reception and briefing, game monitoring, reset and technical work, and two reflection facilitators. Premium workshops, breaks, younger children and specific support needs require their own staffed coverage. A simultaneous school start requires a different reflection roster.

The workshop room has a separate calendar. A premium workshop adds 45 minutes after the standard visit. School holding activities and premium workshops cannot be promised the same space at the same time. A slower 75-minute alternative uses 45 minutes of play, 15 minutes of reset and 15 minutes of buffer. Its cost model pays for the five additional reset minutes.

Sources: M08, M20

Capture selected work with 360-degree video, appropriate wearable or handheld views, drawings and the skilled worker's explanation. Associate each lesson with its component, conditions, tools, steps, checks and result. Useful faults and corrections become part of the learning material.

A labourer can receive the specific lesson for labelling a module, fitting a scenic panel, preparing a reset kit or checking a documented starting state. Licensed and specialist work remains assigned to the people authorised and competent to perform it. A recorded lesson supports the work and its supervision.

### Transcription counts every submitted participant channel

The current official price example is OpenAI's file transcription model, gpt-transcribe, at an estimated US$0.0045 per audio minute. The published live transcription comparison is US$0.017 per minute. [R121][R122]

Chargeable minutes equal sold teams × actual average recorded participants × minutes submitted per participant. The current product mix assumes 5.15 participants and 45 minutes of gameplay. Separate participant files each count for their full submitted duration. This does not assume that silence is free or that six channels are charged as one. The published multichannel billing principle is also explicit in Deepgram's official pricing documentation. [R124]

| Four-room booking case | Sold teams | Submitted audio minutes | File example, US$ | Live example, US$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 30% | 2,726.4 | 631,843.2 | 2,843.29 | 10,741.33 |
| 50% | 4,544.0 | 1,053,072.0 | 4,738.82 | 17,902.22 |
| 70% | 6,361.6 | 1,474,300.8 | 6,634.35 | 25,063.11 |

### Noticing destructive language and useful repair

| Pattern | Illustrative expression | Question for reflection |
|---|---|---|
| Blame | This is all your fault. | What happened, who knew what, and how was responsibility distributed? |
| Contempt or humiliation | You are useless. | What did the personal judgement do to the exchange? |
| Global labels | You always ruin everything. | Which specific event became a claim about the whole person? |
| Dismissal or exclusion | Nobody asked you. | What contribution was attempted and was it considered? |
| Pressure | Do it my way or you are out. | What choices and boundaries were available? |
| Destructive self-talk | I am stupid. | What difficulty became an identity judgement? |
| Premature hopelessness | Nothing will work. | What options and support remained? |
| Certainty that silences | Obviously I am right. | What evidence and alternative explanations were considered? |
| Deflection after harm | It was only a joke. | How did intention, reception and responsibility relate? |

The analysis examines an exchange in context. Anger, criticism, refusal and firm boundaries can be useful and appropriate. Profanity alone does not determine the meaning: 'This is fucking frustrating' expresses frustration, while 'You are fucking useless' directs degradation at a person. Banter, personal language, culture and the participants' accounts matter.

Retain the words, surrounding turns, task state, observed response, possible repair and participants' interpretations. A quiet person may be reading, assembling, observing or thinking. The system identifies candidate moments for review; a facilitator checks attribution and context before discussing them. Participants can correct the record.

Useful progress includes noticing a pattern, making a more specific request, accepting a boundary, hearing a different explanation, repairing harm and using the skill later. The record describes behaviour in a situation and the person's chosen learning.

### Vocabulary that gives people more choices

| Starting expression | Useful distinction | An alternative to rehearse afterwards |
|---|---|---|
| You never listen. | Observation and request | I have not finished the second instruction. Can I finish, then hear your idea? |
| I cannot do anything. | Difficulty and capability | I am stuck on this step. I can explain what I have tried. |
