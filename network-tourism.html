03 · Network and tourism · Luke Nathan Hayes

A tourism network that rewards return visits

Give every destination its own character and every returning group a fresh reason to travel, contribute and learn.

The network product

A visitor can enjoy one room as a complete experience, then choose a linked journey through other participating venues. Each room has a different story, task structure and local connection. The network maintains room and scenario versions so returning groups can find genuinely unfamiliar experiences.

Three product lengths serve different customers: a local day with two experiences and an independent food or cultural stop; a regional weekend with several rooms and accommodation; and a longer passport used across a journey. Employer retreats add a workshop tied to actual work. School series can revisit the same capabilities through different room settings.

Partner businesses publish their actual offer, price, capacity, accessibility and booking arrangements. A package can be sold by a named contracting operator, or customers can buy linked services directly. The selling and settlement model is clear before bookings open.

A proposed three-destination journey

StageExperience and local tradeSkill carried into the next setting
First destinationA practical workshop room, followed by an independently operated lunch or gallery visitShare information, make a clear request and notice assumptions.
Second destinationAn investigation room in another town, with accommodation and local experiences available through partnersCheck evidence, hear another interpretation and revise a plan.
Third destinationA shared-place room and optional final reflectionNegotiate responsibilities, repair an unhelpful exchange and choose a real-life application.

Brisbane, Redlands and Minjerribah provide one route concept for development. Sunshine Coast and hinterland, Northern Rivers and Coffs Harbour, and Melbourne, Beechworth and Albury provide other possible regional patterns. These are development examples. Actual participating venues, ferry or road timetables, accessible transport, cultural hosts and accommodation must be assembled for each published itinerary.

Travel itself creates useful practice: agreeing a meeting time, accommodating different needs, asking for assistance, changing a plan and sharing responsibility. The next room introduces a fresh task where people can use their skills without an in-game reminder.

The direct network pass and settlement

The proposed direct pass is $169 per person for three participating rooms. Its allocation is $53 to each receiving venue and $10 for the network service, all stated as GST-inclusive cash amounts where applicable. Compared with three individual $59 admissions, the pass saves $8 and offers a connected journey.

The venue allocations remain committed to future admissions until the relevant service is delivered or the booking is otherwise resolved under the agreed terms. A venue records its own admission revenue. The network records its service amount under its agreed agency or principal arrangement. Accounting and tax treatment are confirmed in the operating agreements.

For five passes bought in one domestic-card transaction, $845 is collected, $795 is allocated to venue admissions and $50 to the network service. Payment processing and the network's own delivery costs come out of the network amount in this example. The finance chapter shows the calculation and the limits of this tariff.

Wholesale and high-commission channels need a separately priced offer. The direct pass cannot absorb every distribution fee while preserving the same venue payments. Refunds, cancellations, unredeemed admissions, partner departure and failed delivery each have an assigned payer and settlement procedure.

How neighbouring businesses benefit

Accommodation providers gain a reason for an extra night. Cafes and restaurants gain groups with a planned break. Galleries, museums, guides and cultural hosts can offer their own experiences between rooms. Local makers can supply set dressing, props, repairs and original stories. Councils and tourism organisations can help connect venues with precincts, visitor information and relevant regional programmes.

Measure partner benefit through authorised referral codes, actual redeemed bookings and optional group feedback. Report partner sales separately from the escape-room business's revenue. A useful local-impact report includes supplier payments, paid work, room admissions, attributed partner transactions and funded access places.

Participants can retain a chosen learning thread in their personal Aura. Tourism partners receive the booking information needed to deliver their service. Personal reflections are not a condition of cross-venue ticket redemption.

Rotation makes the network more valuable

Mystery Rooms provides a practical precedent for game movement: its current Geelong page describes consolidation at Fitzroy and long-term residencies for two games with operators in Beechworth and Albury. Its vouchers exclude those partner venues, demonstrating why a shared pass needs deliberately negotiated redemption terms.

Our rooms are designed with documented interfaces, transportable components and a version history. A room can gain a new audience in another region while its previous venue receives a fresh experience. The renewal contract accounts for deinstallation, condition checks, freight, receiving-site works, commissioning, training and downtime. The venue and network each earn only the revenue their agreement provides.
