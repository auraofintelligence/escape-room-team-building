Itemised setup and operating economics

Every budget line has a quantity, a price or rate, a reason, a source and a status.

The cost standard

The budget uses three clearly identified types of entry. A published unit price is multiplied by a stated design quantity. A work package uses explained task hours and a published wage or contractor charge-rate comparator. A site-specific item remains marked quotation required until its scope and price are available.

Published retail stock and sale prices are dated. Freight, GST, condition, warranty, installation and access to the actual stock are recorded separately where necessary. A second-hand chair is compared with a suitable new alternative through its delivered and usable cost, including any cleaning or repair.

Subtotals are labelled according to what they include. The model leaves unquoted work visible and calculates what the business could afford to pay for the remaining scope.

The delivery cost record

Each quoted work package identifies the drawing or room version, quantities, materials, labour roles, time, tools, delivery, installation, tests, training, warranty, payment milestones and exclusions. A supplier's game package and a complete Australian premises are different scopes.

New and used items are selected by fitness for the task. Durable tables, chairs, cabinets, desks, storage and scenic objects are useful second-hand candidates. Electrical, structural, fire, access, load-bearing and recording-system components require the specific suitability checks and professional work described in the site brief.

Software and AI unit-cost appendix

Current provider unit prices and explicit usage quantities make the software budget reviewable. The proposed operating roles remain inactive.

Transcription counts every submitted participant channel

The current official price example is OpenAI's file transcription model, gpt-transcribe, at an estimated US$0.0045 per audio minute. The published live transcription comparison is US$0.017 per minute. [R121][R122]

Chargeable minutes equal sold teams × actual average recorded participants × minutes submitted per participant. The current product mix assumes 5.15 participants and 45 minutes of gameplay. Separate participant files each count for their full submitted duration. This does not assume that silence is free or that six channels are charged as one. The published multichannel billing principle is also explicit in Deepgram's official pricing documentation. [R124]

At 50%, the calculation is 4,544 × 5.15 × 45 = 1,053,072 submitted audio minutes. The file example is therefore US$4,738.82 per year. The 5.15 average currently matches paying participants; any recorded teachers, carers or facilitators increase recorded headcount even if they do not buy a ticket. Recording the debrief, retrying failed uploads or submitting duplicate files adds its actual duration. Do not budget promotional credits or a silence reduction before they exist.

Post-play processing is the base example. Check actual turnaround, upload limits, accents, languages and noisy-room performance during the capture pilot before selecting a provider. Silent processing during play can also be tested without providing any in-game response. Actual turnaround determines the useful workflow.

Price each proposed agent by its work

A current standard token-price example is gpt-6.1-sol at US$2 per million uncached input tokens and US$10 per million output tokens. Web search costs US$0.01 per call plus retrieved content tokens; file search costs US$0.0025 per call plus relevant storage and model usage. [R121]

The following two-call reflection package is a proposed usage budget to measure in the pilot. Input budgets include transcript, instructions and rubric; the second pass also includes the first draft. Output is for a qualified human facilitator to review. No cache or batch discount is assumed.

The two-call package totals US$0.092 per team, or US$418.05 for 4,544 teams. Actual long transcripts, more revisions, additional analysis or larger models change the cost. This example does not claim that every team fits the token budget or that analysis is complete without human judgment.

For any provider, calculate each approved role as:

Runs × [(uncached input tokens × input rate + cached reads × cached rate + billable cache writes × write rate + billable output tokens × output rate) ÷ 1,000,000 + tool actions × their individual charges], then add actual compute, storage, connector and licence costs.

Count every model call and retry in a multi-step run. Include billable reasoning output where charged. A proposed planning deliverable using 20,000 uncached input tokens, 4,000 output tokens and three web searches costs US$0.11 at these unit prices, provided retrieved search tokens fit within the stated input budget. Its monthly total is US$0.11 × the approved number of runs, plus any other specified service costs. Monthly role volumes remain unset until Luke approves their actual tasks and limits.

The operating role budget therefore records deliverable, runs, calls per run, input and output tokens, tool actions, retry count, storage and compute units, human review hours, monthly cap and actual spend. Set action permissions separately from spending permissions. A budget for a proposed research role does not authorise contacting contractors, placing orders or publishing commitments.

Currency and remaining software costs

API credits are currently purchased in US dollars. [R123] Convert the actual USD invoice at the actual AUD paid per USD, add payment and foreign-exchange charges and applicable taxes, and deduct only eligible input tax credits. Do not mix the USD appendix directly into the AUD tables.

Booking integration, secure hosting, storage, backup, retention, workflow implementation, testing, maintenance, connector seats and additional human review still need their own quantities and quotes. The facilitator's existing time allocation covers review only if the pilot shows it fits; otherwise add the measured time. The published venue headroom is still before software and these other costs, so it remains a capacity to pay costs rather than a profit claim.
