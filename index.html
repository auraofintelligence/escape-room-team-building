Executive summary

Build a national adventure network that brings visitors to local businesses, turns shared challenges into skills for life, and gives Australians a fair go in the age of superintelligence.

The business

I am building an Australian escape-room network alongside Aura of Intelligence and p4a-xyz-cinema. The business designs and deploys distinctive rooms, helps local operators run them, connects those experiences into tourism routes, and renews the catalogue so groups have a reason to return. Visitors buy an enjoyable shared adventure. Schools and employers can buy a deeper programme, and participating venues gain a route to repeat bookings, new customers and continuing practical support.

I lead the business with a proposed suite of AI agents. Paid labourers, tradespeople, designers, facilitators and specialist contractors carry out the work that needs their skills. The agents prepare research, specifications, schedules, comparisons, records and decision packs. The operating system described in this plan awaits my approval before any role is activated or given authority to contact people, hire, purchase or operate customer services.

Joyful Responsible Abundance gives the business its direction: experiences worth enjoying, responsibility for our effect on other people, and wider access to useful work, learning, ownership and opportunity.

What customers and partners buy

The opening product catalogue has a $39 school and youth visit, a $59 public visit and a $99 facilitated team workshop, all proposed consumer prices including GST. A $169 direct three-room network pass connects different experiences and destinations. The pass is a proposed commercial product, with a specific venue-settlement model later in this plan.

Local businesses can participate through a hosted room, an independently operated venue, a room-deployment contract or a tourism partnership. Co-operatives can own local assets where that suits their members. Accommodation, food, cultural experiences, transport and other activities remain distinct businesses with agreed responsibilities and payments.

The revenue model has three ledgers: local venue admissions and workshops; paid design, installation and renewal contracts; and separately agreed network services and distribution. Each ledger records its own payer, delivery cost and margin. Visitor spending at a partner cafe is valuable local activity and remains that cafe's revenue.

Why this is a commercial opportunity

Australia already has an established escape-room market. The operator sample in this plan ranges from three to fourteen rooms at one venue, alongside larger mixed attractions. Existing operators sell corporate events, multiple-game packages and touring or relocated game content. The opportunity is to combine good rooms, useful reflection, repeat progression, local stories and an intentionally connected visitor network.

Tourism, school excursions, public leisure and workplace development serve different parts of the calendar. Each candidate location gets its own demand case, built from reachable customers, competitor offers, partner accommodation, transport, opening hours and actual bookings. National tourism and education statistics supply context. Deployment follows local commercial cases and available delivery capacity.

The first deployment and the financial approach

The first delivery unit is a two-room hosted pilot with a complete operating and renewal record. A four-room venue is the reference operating case, with a selected 400 m² space programme and up to six paying players per game. Larger class and event formats require their own room count, reflection spaces and staffing.

The cost schedule distinguishes published supplier prices, stated design quantities, work-package hours, current wage benchmarks and items requiring a site-specific quotation. It compares second-hand and new furnishings. The priced subtotal is visible; unquoted work is never entered as zero or disguised inside a broad allowance.

The plan carries the just-in-time learning method from Installing the Missing Middle into room deployment. Each installation produces a spatial record, asset register, reset procedure, commissioning evidence and short lessons for the next paid crew. Renewal is designed into the room, the cash model and the contract from the beginning.

The development decision

The immediate decision is whether to adopt the proposed operating system and authorise its first defined work packages. The decision pack covers the role charters, account access, contractor engagement, expenditure limits, external communications and evidence required for acceptance. Site selection and purchase commitments follow their own completed cost and delivery packs.

This publication provides the full business scope, market evidence, itemised cost basis, tourism settlement design, agent responsibilities and staged programme. No operating agent has been activated by publishing it.
