Finance, settlements and local ownership

Fund the work, pay people properly, preserve renewal cash and make each participant's return understandable.

Three connected accounts

AccountIncomeCosts and obligations
Local venueIts own admissions, workshops and agreed local servicesPeople, premises, delivery, maintenance, renewal, finance and applicable tax.
Deployment businessAgreed design, installation, commissioning, training and upgrade contractsMaterials, paid labour, contractors, freight, project work and warranty obligations.

A consolidated network report eliminates transfers between related entities. An independent partner's revenue stays with that partner. A room sale and the operator's later ticket sales are different transactions at different times.

Customer money committed to future services is tracked through the pass-settlement ledger. Grants, loans, equity, member capital and customer receipts each retain their actual financial character. An expression of interest and a guarantee are not cash receipts.

Products, available sessions and the sales mix

The proposed prices are $39 for school and youth, $59 for public groups and $99 for the premium workshop, including GST. The scenario uses booking shares of 30%, 50% and 20%, with respective paid group sizes of 6, 4.5 and 5.5. The weighted average is 5.15 paid participants and $311.85 in receipts per sold team, or $283.50 excluding GST.

The four-room year has 9,216 nominal team sessions. One planned closed trading day per room each quarter removes 128 slots, leaving 9,088 available sessions. The 48-week trading calendar separately leaves four weeks for closures and major work. Further downtime reduces the calendar when it occurs.

Published wage inputs and paid task time

The current Amusement, Events and Recreation Award publishes adult casual Grade 4 rates of $36.81 for ordinary hours and $51.54 for Sunday, and Grade 6 rates of $39.11 and $54.76. ATO states a 12% super guarantee rate. These two wage bands are transparent examples for estimating the roster; actual award coverage, classification, agreed pay, overtime and allowances are determined from each job. [R101, R102]

For a six-day pattern with five ordinary days and one Sunday, the weighted rates including super are $43.9768 and $46.724533 per hour. These are wages plus super, not fully loaded contractor rates.

The reference staffing calculation pays for two baseline roles across 11 hours a day. Across 288 days that is 3,168 hours per role. Additional paid time for each team is itemised as 35 minutes of reflection preparation, delivery and notes at the second rate, and 25 minutes of reset, handover and relief at the first rate. Premium bookings add 45 minutes at the second rate. The booking mix gives $52.588324 in additional wages plus super per sold team.

A peak operating roster still needs reception, dedicated monitoring, reset work and two overlapping reflection facilitators, plus a premium facilitator when booked. The annual task allowance must be converted to real shifts with breaks, minimum engagements, school supervision and access support. Any additional paid shift time is an added cost. Management, Luke's programme work, insurance, payroll tax where applicable, training and specialist contractor charges are separate.

Coverage hours and roster construction

The labour calculation buys coverage and defined work. It does not assume that a person works an eleven-hour ordinary casual shift. The four-room baseline provides one monitor/control position and one host/operations position for eleven hours each trading day, shared across a roster. The extra task minutes are paid in addition to that baseline. At the 50% booking case, they reconcile as follows.

Under the award used as the published wage benchmark, casual ordinary hours are limited to ten per day and thirty-eight per week. The usual casual minimum engagement is three hours; Sunday work has a four-hour minimum. Final coverage, classification, breaks and shift construction need an actual roster. [R101]

This is why the model does not promise that 35 task minutes can be purchased as an isolated 35-minute shift. Cluster bookings, schedule minimum-length shifts, and measure the difference between task hours and paid hours. Add simultaneous demand, break coverage beyond the relief minutes, child and accessibility support, management and other named duties where needed. These differences remain visible in the quote register, rather than being called free founder labour.

The 75-minute cycle means 45 minutes of independent play, 15 minutes of reset and 15 minutes of timetable buffer. It adds five paid reset minutes per sold team to the standard case and reduces the offer to seven starts per room per day. The game remains 45 minutes.

A complete break-even calculation becomes available once those costs are entered. The formula is annual fixed costs and renewal provision divided by contribution per sold team, then divided by available annual slots. Contribution includes all variable labour, consumables, distribution and payment fees.

Tourism pass: settlement without double counting

A proposed direct three-room pass is $169 per person including GST, compared with three ordinary $59 visits at $177. Allocate $53 to each of three venue redemptions and $10 to the network service. Each venue knows its realised tariff before accepting pass inventory. This is a proposed tariff for approval.

The $795 venue allocation is held for venue settlement. Unredeemed entitlements remain separately tracked; the network cannot treat that money as earned revenue. The central service earns approximately $6.50 per pass after the example processing charge, before support, software, marketing, refunds and administration. Without claiming GST credit on processor fees, the five-pass amount is $31.21. [R103]

At 1,000 passes sold in groups of five, the same tariff produces approximately $6,501 after processing and before other network costs. This demonstrates why the central business also needs properly priced design, installation, training, maintenance and optional service contracts. A large aggregate ticket flow does not itself fund the whole central operation.

An agent or online distributor commission would consume part of this small direct service margin. Build a separate wholesale tariff or negotiate a separately priced marketing service. Do not silently stack a second commission onto the same direct pass.

Rooms on an itinerary should offer different challenges and local stories. Booking availability, travel time and partner opening days determine the route. Accommodation, meals, transport, cultural experiences and other attractions retain their own prices, payments and responsibilities. Count the additional visits and local spending observed through agreed referral records; do not count all tourism spending as escape-network revenue. Skill transfer follows the participant's chosen learning across different rooms without requiring broad sharing of their private recordings.

Capital that supports continuing local capability

Funding can combine founder capital, host contributions, customer contracts, debt, co-operative member capital, public-benefit funding and separately negotiated investment. Each proposed source is tied to an eligible applicant, a defined use and its actual terms. The initial cash plan does not assume a grant award or a partner commitment.

Where external capital finances a room for later local acquisition, define its purchase basis, repayment, agreed return, end point and ownership transfer. The deployment margin, investor return and operating surplus are recorded separately. A co-operative can acquire its room or venue assets while continuing to buy network services on agreed terms.

Renewal money belongs in the local operating case from opening. Paying off the first fitout does not fund a later replacement automatically. A new upgrade has its own scope, price and ownership record.

Cash timing and commercial protection

The cash schedule follows supplier deposits, delivery, site works, commissioning, payroll, booking receipts, partner settlement, refunds, taxes and renewal funding. Milestone amounts come from the accepted contracts. Once the remaining scopes and payment terms are quoted, the completed cash forecast will show the cash required before opening and the lowest cash balance during the sales ramp.

A staged contract can separate paid discovery, detailed design, procurement, installation, acceptance and support. Each stage has an output that can be reviewed and accepted. Variations identify their cost and effect on the timetable before the work proceeds under the agreed authority.

C-Hour remains non-monetary recognition of voluntary contribution. Paid work, investor returns and ownership interests use their own agreements and accounts. Voluntary participation does not supply an assumed wage saving in the commercial budget.
