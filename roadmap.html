ROUND ONE · 11 · Research and next decisions

Research questions and possible development sequence

Use the evidence to decide what is worth developing next, with timing derived from the work rather than an assumed opening date.

A possible sequence of research and development

A possible sequence is set out below for comparing dependencies. There are no assigned weeks, opening date or committed pilot duration.

Research or development questionEvidence that would make a later decision concrete
Which customers and offers warrant further work?Local demand evidence, competitor conditions and responses to alternative offers.
Which room and venue formats fit the opportunity?Measured support-space needs, room concepts, access requirements and capacity comparisons.
What would delivery actually cost?Comparable scope, new/used options, dated quotations, paid-work estimates and payment terms.
Which learning and technical methods are useful?Playtests, capture comparisons, human review time and evidence of use in a different setting.
Would a small operating test be worthwhile?A defined question, feasible staffing, costed alternatives and criteria for interpreting the result.
What would justify another location or route?Evidence of customer value, repeat use, viable local economics and workable partner arrangements.

For one supplier comparison, the published production lead times are 60 working days for standard rooms and 90 for custom rooms, with shipping additional. Those are source-specific observations dated 29 October 2026, not this business's timetable. Actual timing would depend on the route chosen and its delivery evidence. [M14]

What an operating test could establish

A pilot is an option for answering defined questions, rather than the assumed first purchase. It could test a room concept, a host arrangement, an operating format or a recording workflow. Its scale and duration would depend on what needs to be learned.

Useful observations could include room and support-space measurements, itemised delivered costs, paid task time, resets, independent play, customer response and reflection quality. A technical test would also need capture performance, processing time and human review workload. A public, school or workshop format would need to be evaluated on its own terms.

An operating trial could compare contribution by product and staffed opening block, repeat interest and tourism referrals that become visits. The decision to expand would require more than a successful demonstration: it would need a repeatable delivery method and a complete local cost case.

Questions about national expansion

The Australia-wide aim can be explored without choosing an initial region or site count. A location comparison would bring together demand, premises, people, finance, room content and the value of connections with other destinations.

A later deployment could test whether another paid crew can use the first installation's records and JIT learning material. An inter-venue trial could examine booking, redemption, settlement, customer support and skill transfer into a different experience. Local stories and relationships would need their own development.

Three-, five- and ten-year views can be used as comparison horizons for asset replacement, renewal funding, ownership possibilities and regional coverage. They are analytical views, not a selected expansion programme or a commitment to dates.

The next research round

The next research round can follow the questions Luke considers most useful: market demand, room design, delivery formats, new and used cost options, learning transfer, tourism connections or commercial structure. The accompanying register keeps evidence gaps and choices visible without assigning the founder an assumed next decision.

A later financial comparison would replace unverified listings and assumed quantities with better evidence, then show complete cash requirements and operating results for the remaining alternatives. No total should depend on hiding unpriced work or treating founder time as free.
