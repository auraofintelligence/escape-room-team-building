These windows describe work sequencing. The opening date is set after the site, production and delivery path are chosen. The supplier comparison lists 60 working days for standard production or 90 for custom production, with freight additional and most installations taking four to seven days after arrival. Those lead times can govern the critical path. They cannot be compressed into the early design weeks by labelling the entire project a short pilot.

What the pilot must deliver

The pilot is a complete small operation. Its accepted record includes the actual room and support-space measurements, published offer, itemised delivered costs, paid roster, session timetable, reset process, no-hint playtesting, audio performance, reflection method, booking and settlement records, maintenance and renewal schedule.

Run school, public and workshop bookings through their actual formats. Measure time spent fitting equipment, briefing, monitoring, resetting, reviewing exchanges, reflecting, cleaning, maintaining and supporting participants. Include cancellations, technical interruptions and customer service in the workload record.

Review contribution by product and staffed opening block. Track repeat bookings and which tourism referrals result in actual visits. Replace design quantities and task hours with observed values as they become available. Record why a proposed expansion improves the combined business.

From one deployment to a national network

Expansion follows ready opportunities and crew capacity. Each new location needs a measured site, an accountable operator, a local demand case, an accepted budget, trained people, a room catalogue and an agreed network relationship. The number of sites is an output of those cases.

The second deployment should use the first crew's documented work and demonstrate that another team can install, reset and operate the room. The first inter-venue route should demonstrate booking, redemption, payment, customer support and transfer into a different experience. Subsequent regions can add their own stories and partners while using the shared records and service standards.

A rolling three-year programme tracks design releases, deployment work, customer growth, asset age, planned renewal, funding and paid workforce development. Five- and ten-year views examine replacement cycles, ownership transitions, regional coverage and the continuing relevance of the offer. Each view is revised from actual operations.

The approval pack and next deliverables

The next review considers the operating role charters, initial account access, communication authority, contractor process and expenditure limits. The first delivery pack then brings together the selected site, two-room specification, measured quantities, new/used procurement comparisons, actual quotes, schedule, cash requirements and acceptance criteria.

Unresolved inputs have a responsible role and a defined output. Site-dependent costs remain visible until priced. Proposed partner participation remains distinct from an agreement. The business can progress several preparation streams in parallel while preserving those distinctions.

The deliverables at this stage are the full business plan, matching website and portable editions, source collection, itemised financial basis, role charters and approval register. The operating system awaits Luke's decision.
