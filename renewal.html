Renewal, rotation and useful asset life

Compare renewal and rotation methods that could keep rooms useful, support repeat visits and make future costs visible.

Worked maintenance and closure assumptions

  • Each reset: recorded prop count, mechanism and safety checks, microphone cleaning/charge, version confirmation and test state. Time is already in the room cycle; consumables follow the actual supplier bill.
  • Monthly illustration: an assumed two technical hours per room for a detailed mechanical inspection and repairs. Across four rooms this is 96 hours a year. At the published shopfitting range of $65-$85/hour, the labour component is $6,240-$8,160 excluding GST, with parts and electronics specialists separate. [R104]
  • Quarterly illustration: one assumed blocked trading day per room for a version change and fresh-group check. The 128 unavailable sessions are already removed from annual capacity. Each update gets a task brief and content/technical quote.
  • Year 3: review repeat demand, content saturation, wear, remaining support and the return from repair, new puzzle paths, room exchange or retirement.
  • Years 4-5: compare technology and physical renewal needs against the asset-specific provision. Quote freight, reinstallation and recommissioning when rooms move.
  • Years 8-10: repeat technology review, inspect the long-lived partitions/services, revisit access and the room mix, and prepare the next capital schedule. Useful parts can move to another location after safety, data handling, support and receiving-site checks.

A content renewal brief can be priced as explicit deliverables: revised story/clue set, alternate route, accessible version, no-hint playtest, facilitator notes, updated reset guide and JIT lesson. The writer, educator, prop maker and testing time each receive a quantity and quoted rate. It is not hidden within an unexplained annual content allowance.

Content renewal and room rotation

Create several scenario variants around durable mechanisms. Change evidence, roles, dependencies and the final decision so a returning participant encounters a new task. Preserve the room's reset reliability and learning purpose as the story changes.

When a room has a stronger audience elsewhere, prepare a rotation proposal. Record the module condition, ownership, receiving layout, changes, transport dimensions, freight quote, installation labour, commissioning, training, downtime and settlement. A room relocation is a costed project with an accountable receiving operator.

A future network catalogue could show where each version is operating. Customers can choose new experiences without a private learning record being shared across the network. The asset record tracks the parts; the participant chooses which learning continues with them.

Pay for downtime as well as parts

A renewal schedule includes closed room sessions and the staff or host costs that continue while work happens. Where feasible, rotate one room at a time and use quieter periods. Temporary reductions in room capacity also affect school and corporate booking limits.

Calculate the cash effect using the sessions displaced, expected sold share, actual group size, net ticket yield and avoidable delivery costs. Avoid treating every unavailable slot as a sold ticket, and avoid treating a closed room as cost-free.

The reserve would need to remain visible in an operating forecast. An actual renewal would draw on it against its costed scope. A new design release updates the spatial record, parts list, reset procedure, learner material and receiving crew's task pack.
